### 2. Audit Compliance Edits — mNC 14 (Trending, Review Frequency, Escalation)

Finding: mNC 14 — The procedure defines a limited number of quality objectives and does not establish requirements for trending methods, review frequency, or escalation criteria. The current objectives do not ensure comprehensive coverage or consistent monitoring of quality system performance.

The current document (Rev A) contains only a Purpose, Scope, Definitions, and a two-row Quality Objectives table. The additions below expand the SOP to include procedural requirements and finalized objectives.

Change 1 of 2 — Add procedural requirements section before the Quality Objectives table
- Location: Insert a new section between the Definitions section and the current Quality Objectives table.
- Formatting note: Style the line "Procedure: Quality Objective Management Requirements" as a main section heading. Style "Trending Methods," "Review Frequency," and "Escalation Criteria" as sub-headings one level below. The remaining lines are body paragraphs; the indented examples may be formatted as bulleted lists in Word.
- Add the following new section:

```
Procedure: Quality Objective Management Requirements

Trending Methods

Quality performance data for each quality objective shall be tracked on at least a quarterly basis. The trending method for each objective shall be appropriate to the data type:

For rate-based objectives (lot acceptance rate, complaint rate): Trend the monthly or quarterly rate over time. Evaluate the rolling twelve-month average against the defined goal and note any directional trend (improving, stable, or declining) relative to prior periods.

For activity-count objectives (active post-market surveillance activities, employee training program activities): Track the number of qualifying activities completed during the calendar year against the annual target, and report progress against the pace required to meet the annual target.

For completion-rate objectives (execution of quality plan objectives): Track the number of action items on or ahead of schedule versus the total reviewed in each quarterly period. Report the percentage on or ahead of schedule.

Trend data for each objective shall be compiled into a Quality Objectives Status Report on at least a quarterly basis. The report shall be prepared by the quality objective Owner identified in the Quality Objectives table and submitted to the Management Representative.

Review Frequency

Quality objectives shall be reviewed at the following intervals:

Quarterly review: The Management Representative shall review the Quality Objectives Status Report each quarter. The purpose of the quarterly review is to identify objectives that are trending unfavorably and to initiate timely corrective action before the annual management review.

Annual review: Quality objectives and their goals shall be formally reviewed at the annual management review meeting under QM.SLQ018. At the annual review, the management team shall confirm whether goals remain appropriate, adjust them based on historical performance and business context, and establish or retire objectives as needed.

Escalation Criteria

A quality objective shall be escalated to a CAPA evaluation under QM.SLQ016 Corrective and Preventive Action SOP when it falls short of its goal as follows: the incoming material lot acceptance rate falls below 85 percent for two consecutive quarters; the finished product complaint rate reaches or exceeds 1 percent of distributed catheters in any rolling twelve-month period; an annual activity-count objective (Active Post-Market Surveillance or Dynamic Employee Training Program) is tracking behind the pace required to meet its annual target, evidenced by fewer than half of the annual target completed by the end of the second quarter; or the Quarterly Execution of Quality Plan Objectives falls below 80 percent at any quarterly review.

Any quality objective that has not been actively monitored for two consecutive quarterly periods due to missing data shall trigger a data collection corrective action and be reported at the next management review.

Any objective for which the defined goal is consistently met but management determines the goal is insufficiently rigorous shall be flagged for revision at the next annual management review.

The CAPA initiated from a quality objective escalation shall reference the specific objective, the trend data supporting escalation, and the escalation criteria met.
```

### 2. Audit Compliance Edits — mNC 15 (Universal Documentation, Trending, CAPA Linkage, Use-as-Is Risk Assessment)

Finding: mNC 15 — The procedure allows certain nonconformances (expected inspection fallout) to be handled without formal documentation. It does not explicitly link nonconformance data to CAPA evaluation or require documented risk assessment for use-as-is dispositions.

Change 1 of 3 — Revise the paragraph allowing undocumented handling of expected fallout
- Location: Section "Procedure: Nonconforming Material Identification" — the paragraph beginning "A Non-conforming Material Report is not always required..."
- Current text (verbatim):

```
A Non-conforming Material Report is not always required for product that has been rejected for not meeting a pre-defined inspection criteria during receiving, quality control, or in-process inspections.  This specifically applies to cases where failures are observed that are expected for certain manufacturing yields.   A Non-conforming Material Report is still required however for product rejected for reasons other than criteria defined in the inspection procedures or if the number of failures is higher than expected yields.
```

- Replace with:

```
A Non-conforming Material Report (NCMR) shall be completed for all product that does not conform to applicable requirements or specifications, including product rejected during receiving inspection, in-process quality control inspection, and final quality control inspection. This requirement applies regardless of whether the failure was anticipated as part of normal manufacturing yields or inspection fallout.

Where a product-specific inspection procedure defines an expected yield or a process-specific nonconformance rate that is documented and approved in the applicable DMR or inspection procedure, individual units rejected within that expected range may be disposed of per the specific inspection procedure (e.g., scrap without a separate NCMR) provided that: (a) the inspection procedure explicitly defines the allowable disposition for within-yield failures; (b) the total quantity of failures per lot is recorded in a form that is available for trending (e.g., on the inspection record or in the NCMR Log FM2-QM.SLQ040); and (c) an NCMR is still required if the number of failures in any single lot exceeds the expected yield defined in the inspection procedure or DMR, or if the failure mode is outside the scope of expected yield.

All nonconformance data, including within-yield failures recorded on inspection records, shall be available for trending review during management review and CAPA evaluation activities.
```

Change 3 of 3 — Add risk assessment requirement for use-as-is dispositions
- Location: Section "Procedure: Nonconforming Material Disposition" — the description of the Use As Is disposition
- Current text (verbatim):

```
(U) Use as is:  Reported non-conformance is acceptable for use "as is" in final product.  If the defect is found to be invalid and the non-conformance does not affect the form, fit, or function of product's intended use, is cosmetically acceptable, and has no health or safety implications.  This disposition may require an approved Temporary Deviation or an approved Permanent Change to the specification.
```

- Replace with:

```
(U) Use as is: Reported non-conformance is acceptable for use "as is" in final product, subject to the following requirements. Use-as-is disposition shall not be applied unless: (a) the nonconformance does not affect the form, fit, or function of the product's intended use; (b) the nonconformance presents no health or safety risk to the patient or user; and (c) a documented risk assessment has been completed by an authorized individual and recorded in Section C of FM1-QM.SLQ040. The risk assessment shall evaluate the potential patient safety and clinical performance impact of the nonconformance and shall confirm that the residual risk of use-as-is is acceptable. A use-as-is disposition shall not be approved by Quality Assurance without a completed risk assessment. This disposition may additionally require an approved Temporary Deviation or an approved Permanent Change to the specification.
```

### 2. Audit Compliance Edits — mNC 10 (Objective Validation Criteria)

Finding: mNC 10 — The scope contains discretionary language ("when necessary") that does not clearly define conditions requiring process validation. The procedure does not require documented justification when validation is not performed.

Change 1 of 2 — Revise Scope to replace discretionary language with objective criteria
- Location: Section Scope — first paragraph
- Current text (verbatim):

```
This procedure, when necessary, applies to manufacturing related processes and equipment/fixtures used to manufacture product at SILQ manufacturing facilities.  It allows process validation to be performed by a certified contract services provider, such as packaging and sterilization validations.
```

- Replace with:

```
This procedure applies to manufacturing-related processes and equipment / fixtures used to manufacture product at SILQ manufacturing facilities where the output of the process cannot be fully verified by subsequent inspection, monitoring, or measurement, or where deficiencies in the process output would become apparent only after the product is in use.

Examples of processes that shall be evaluated for validation applicability include, but are not limited to: sterilization processes, coating or surface treatment application processes, packaging seal processes, and any production process where 100% inspection of the output is not performed or is not capable of detecting all relevant nonconformances.

This procedure allows process validation to be performed by a certified contract services provider, such as packaging and sterilization validations. Validation requirements established by SILQ shall be flowed down to the contract services provider through the quality agreement.
```

Change 2 of 2 — Add documented justification requirement when validation is not performed
- Location: Section Procedure — add a new sub-section at the beginning of the Procedure section, before the existing "Where the results of a process cannot be fully verified..." paragraph.
- Current text (verbatim — first sentence of Procedure):

```
Where the results of a process cannot be fully verified by subsequent inspection and test, the process shall be validated.
```

- Formatting note: Style the line "Validation Applicability Determination" as a Procedure sub-section heading matching the other Procedure headings in QM.SLQ047. The remaining lines are body paragraphs; the four objective criteria may be formatted as a bulleted list in Word.
- Add the following new sub-section immediately before:

```
Validation Applicability Determination

For each manufacturing process identified in the applicable Device Master Record or process validation plan, Operations and Quality Assurance shall jointly determine whether process validation is required. The determination shall be documented in the process validation plan or in a standalone Validation Applicability Determination record, and shall be approved by Quality Assurance.

A process validation is required when one or more of the following objective criteria are met:

The output of the process cannot be fully verified by subsequent inspection, monitoring, or measurement under reasonable production conditions.

Deficiencies in the process output are not detectable until the product is in use.

100% inspection of the output is not performed and sampling inspection is not capable of detecting all nonconformances attributable to the process.

The process involves sterilization, surface treatment application, packaging sealing, or another special process identified in the applicable regulatory or quality standard as requiring validation.

When a determination is made that process validation is not required for a specific process, this determination and its basis shall be documented in the process validation plan or Validation Applicability Determination record. The documented justification shall identify the process, state the objective basis for determining validation is not required, and be approved by Quality Assurance before the determination is considered final. Lack of documentation of a not-required determination is not acceptable.
```

- Regulatory basis: ISO 13485:2016 clause 7.5.6 Validation of processes for production and service provision; 21 CFR Part 820 (QMSR) Section 820.75 Process validation; IA-2025 mNC 10.

## Appendix A: Finalized Quality Objectives Reference (QM.SLQ037)
