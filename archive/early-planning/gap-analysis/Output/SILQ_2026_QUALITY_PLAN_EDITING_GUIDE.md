## Overview

This guide provides the complete structure and recommended text for the Silq 2026 Quality Plan. The body is organized by ISO 13485:2016 clause (Sections 4 through 8), with each action item presented as a concise line item that states the problem, the action to address it, the owner, and the target date. Every item is traceable to either the 2025 Management Review Meeting (MR) or the QMS Gap Analysis Report (Gap Analysis).

The action items below are formatted to match the document heading structure. Each action item uses:
- Heading 2 for the item title with owner and target in parentheses
- Heading 3 for Problem and Action Item lines

Text within each action item can be copied directly into the document.

## Purpose (Heading 1)

This quality plan defines the quality practices, resources, and activities to be executed by Silq in 2026. It consolidates action items from the 2025 Management Review Meeting, reconciliation actions from a QMS Gap Analysis (FDA QMSR and ISO 13485:2016), and ongoing regulatory compliance activities.

Per QM.SLQ025 A, this plan addresses changes to the quality system, identification of training requirements, review and updates to risk management processes, preparation for internal and external assessments, and identification of feedback mechanisms for continuous quality improvement.

---

## Background (Heading 1)

The FDA Quality Management System Regulation (QMSR) took effect February 2, 2026, replacing the legacy 21 CFR Part 820 framework with ISO 13485:2016 as the foundational QMS requirement. QMS Gap Analysis identified 2 significant gaps, 5 minor gaps, and a 19-document legacy citation refresh campaign. The 2025 Management Review concluded the quality system is effective, suitable, and adequate with opportunities for improvement identified.

The FDA conducted a Level-2 Surveillance Inspection on October 14-16, 2025 (FEI: 3014650073), resulting in two Form 483 observations. CAPA 2025-001 (Observation 1, MDR reporting) and CAPA 2025-003 (Observation 2, design control for unauthorized valve modification) are in process. Design control project DC.SLQ001 was established as Corrective Action #2 of CAPA 2025-003 to retrospectively apply design controls per QM.SLQ004 B Section 16. A second design control project, DC.SLQ002, manages the validation and transition of the SilqQMS EDMS platform from FileHold per QM.SLQ032.

This plan sequences QMS procedure revisions, CAPA closure activities, training initiatives, and quality objective enhancements to maintain compliance through the transition period.

## Section 4 -- Quality Management System (Heading 1)

---

### QMSR Supplementary Provision Mapping (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q2 2026) [Tier 2, Gap] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: QM.SLQ027 (Quality Manual) does not explicitly integrate the QMSR supplementary provisions (section 820.10(b), section 820.35, section 820.45) as "applicable regulatory requirements." An auditor reviewing the QMS top-level documents would not find a single cross-reference tying each provision to the implementing Silq procedure. Additionally, combination-product applicability (21 CFR 4) has not been formally documented. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Add a QMSR supplementary provision mapping section to QM.SLQ027 that maps section 820.10(b) requirements (UDI, MDR, corrections/removals, combination products) to their implementing procedures, and include a formal combination-product applicability determination statement. (Heading 3)

---

### Medical Device File Framework (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q2 2026) [Tier 1, Gap] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: ISO 13485 clause 4.2.3 requires a "medical device file" as a single defined construct that contains or references all documentation needed to demonstrate device conformity. This concept was not in the old Part 820. Silq maintains the underlying components -- DMR (QM.SLQ048), DHF (design control SOPs), DHR (QM.SLQ029) -- but there is no explicit framework tying them together under the ISO construct. An auditor asking for the "medical device file" would not find a single defined reference. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Revise QM.SLQ027 or QM.SLQ048 to include a "Medical Device File" section. Define the construct and provide a cross-reference mapping existing DMR, DHF, DHR, risk management file, labeling documentation, and product specifications to clause 4.2.3. The underlying content already exists -- this is a framing exercise. (Heading 3)

---

### Legacy 820 Citation Refresh (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q3 2026) [Tier 3, Gap] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: 19 QM documents contain explicit references to old Part 820 section numbers (e.g., section 820.40, section 820.30, section 820.198). These are editorial issues -- the underlying procedures are functional but the regulatory citations are outdated following the QMSR transition. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Execute a controlled batch update through the DCO process to replace old Part 820 citations with the corresponding ISO 13485 clause and QMSR supplementary provision references. The complete list of affected documents and their updated citations is provided in Section 7 of the QMS Gap Analysis Report. (Heading 3)

---

### QMS Platform Transition, DC.SLQ002 (Owner: Ethan Rao, Per DC.SLQ002 Project Schedule) [MR] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: Silq's current EDMS (FileHold) has functional limitations that prompted the transition to SilqQMS, a custom-developed web application. The SilqQMS platform must be validated per QM.SLQ032 and ISO 13485 clause 4.1.6 before production use, and all FileHold-specific procedure references must be updated. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Design control project DC.SLQ002 manages validation of SilqQMS and the controlled platform transition. The project plan (DC.SLQ002 A) defines a phased approach: software validation (SW.SLQ007-012 deliverable series), then procedure-by-procedure migration from FileHold to SilqQMS. The 2026 QMS Transition Plan will be distributed to the management team. (Heading 3)

### Feedback Governance Procedure (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q2 2026) [Tier 1, Gap] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: ISO 13485 clause 8.2.1 requires a documented feedback procedure that provides early warning of quality problems and feeds into risk management. Silq has the underlying pieces -- complaint handling (QM.SLQ021), post-market surveillance (QM.SLQ033), management review (QM.SLQ018), and CAPA (QM.SLQ016) -- but no single governance statement tying them together as a unified feedback system with defined escalation triggers. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Add a governance section to QM.SLQ033 (Post-Market Surveillance SOP) that explicitly defines how complaint data (QM.SLQ021), CAPA trend data (QM.SLQ016), and management review outputs (QM.SLQ018) feed into the feedback loop, and define what constitutes an escalation trigger. This leverages all existing SOPs -- it does not require a new standalone procedure. (Heading 3)

## Risk Management (ISO 14971 / ISO 13485 clause 7.1) (Heading 1)

---

### Post-Production Risk File Update Triggers (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q3 2026) [Tier 2, Gap] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: QM.SLQ012 Section 14 requires evaluation of post-production information for impact on risk management but does not define explicit trigger criteria for when such information requires a formal risk file revision versus monitoring until the next scheduled review. ISO 14971 clauses 10.3-10.4 expect this decision framework to be more explicitly defined. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Add a short decision framework to QM.SLQ012 Section 14 (or to QM.SLQ033) that defines specific trigger criteria for risk file revision -- e.g., new hazard identified, change in severity or probability of a known hazard, safety-relevant regulatory alert, or adverse trend in post-market data. (Heading 3)

---

### Failure Mode and Probability Rating Review (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q2 2026) [MR] (Heading 2)

#### Problem: The 2024 and 2025 Management Reviews both identified a need to evaluate whether existing failure modes accurately reflect all observed product complaint root causes, and whether existing probability ratings should increase based on field complaint experience. Existing hazard #10.1 covers three failure modes with probability currently rated at "1" (Improbable) despite five valve-related complaints over 3 years. (Heading 3)

#### Action Item: Review RM-0018, RM-0019, RM-0020, RM-0021, RM-0094, and RM-0141 against historical product complaint files. Evaluate if failure modes are comprehensive and if probability ratings are appropriately calibrated to field experience. (Heading 3)

---

### ASTM F623-25 Risk Management Alignment (Owner: Ethan Rao, Q3 2026) [MR] (Heading 2)
